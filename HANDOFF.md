**How it moves** (`Movement/ship_movement.gd`, `ShipMovement`):
- **Reaction delay:** `move_to()` and `stop()` only receive a command into one first-in-first-out queue, due `reaction_delay` (0.3 s) later on Movement's physics clock. At the start of each physics tick every due command activates in receipt order: a move sets the destination and the course from where the ship is then; a stop ends the order (ordinary braking). Nothing is cancelled or skipped, and stop has no bypass. Delay 0 acts on the next tick.
- **One velocity:** one world planar velocity and one heading.
- **Frigate values (owner, commit 97ecab7):** mass 12, main acceleration 0.5 m/s², thrusters 40 (40 / 12 = 3.33 m/s²), turn-rate limit 55°/s at full speed, rudder curve (0, 0.05), (0.5, 1), (1, 1.5); the rest are script defaults (15 m/s, 0.25 s response, arrival 2 m / 0.5 m/s, turn authority 120, retro on, 0.45, 0.3 s delay). The numbers below use them.
- **Pilot:** each physics tick it wants to move toward the destination at `sqrt(2 · a_plan · distance) − a_plan · 0.25` m/s, between 0 and 15 (Task #41). Subtracting `a_plan × pilot_response_time` removes the 0.25 s lag of its own response, so it brakes on the ideal stopping curve. Here `a_plan` = the thrusters' reach against the actual travel direction at the actual speed `/ (1 + turning demand fraction)` (bow-first travel: 3.33, so straight braking is unchanged; sideways drift: the faded sideways limit; stern-first travel: the faded forward limit, at most 0.25 m/s²), e.g. bow-first: 3.33 m/s² with no turn, 1.67 m/s² while turning takes its full share. Without retro thrusters it is further limited to `1.5 / s`, where `s` is the actual travel direction's share along the bow (when positive): 1.5 m/s² moving bow-first, 3.33 sideways or stern-first. It requests the acceleration that closes the gap to the actual velocity in 0.25 s.
- **Main engine:** supplies the forward part along the bow, up to 0.5 m/s². It never fires while the ship has any stern-first motion, so it never brakes.
- **Thrusters:** supply the rest from one 40 / 12 = 3.33 m/s² rating, shared with turning (below). **Speed-dependent directional thrust:** sideways thrust is limited to 3.33 × the frigate's `directional_thrust_curve` at the **actual** speed: 100% / 75% / 50% / 25% at 0 / 5 / 10 / 15 m/s (3.33 / 2.50 / 1.67 / 0.83 m/s², linear between, owner-approved provisional). Bow-forward thrust has its own, smaller limit: `forward_directional_acceleration` 0.25 m/s² × the same curve (0.25 / 0.1875 / 0.125 / 0.0625 m/s²), so the 0.5 m/s² main engine is the primary forward propulsion. Sternward thrust keeps the full 3.33 m/s² (then the retro cap). Within the planar share, braking against the actual travel is served first and sideways correction gets only what is left; nothing is scaled up. Momentum is never rotated: at cruise the hull can turn but the ship drifts on.
- **Forward together:** engine and forward thrusters add up to 0.75 m/s² at rest (owner-approved Option A, engine first), falling with the curve to 0.5 + 0.0625 = 0.5625 m/s² at 15 m/s; from rest to 14.9 m/s takes about 23 s and 179 m.
- **Integration:** only applied acceleration is integrated, and the speed is hard-capped at 15 m/s.
- **Turning:** the pilot steers the bow toward the order's **course heading**, set once when the move activates, toward the click; overshoot corrections never change it, a new order replaces it. Authority is `120 / 12 = 10°/s²` × the rudder curve of **actual** speed: 0.05× at rest, 1× at 7.5 m/s, 1.5× at 15 m/s. It winds up and counter-turns with bounded angular acceleration (braking-distance rule), planning the counter-turn on the share it gets beside last tick's thruster demand: `authority / (1 + last planar fraction)`. The turn rate is limited to 55°/s at 15 m/s, scaled down by the same curve (36.7°/s at 7.5 m/s, 1.83°/s at rest). Yaw never rotates the velocity. Without an order the turn rate is 0, so rotation never persists without powered turning. Because the curve is 0.05 at rest, a ship with an order turns slowly in place (0.5°/s², up to 1.83°/s).
- **Shared capacity:** thruster and turning demand are fractions of their ratings; when they sum above 1, both are scaled proportionally. At full demand on both, crabbing/braking gets 1.67 m/s² and turning half its authority. The main engine is outside this.
- **Retro thrusters** (`retro_thrusters`, default true): without them, the thrusters' acceleration toward the stern is capped at `0.45 × 3.33 = 1.5 m/s²` after sharing; only the excess sternward part is removed (lateral and bow-forward thrust are unchanged, nothing is scaled up). This weakens braking from bow-first travel **and** stern-first acceleration (owner-provisional: "Yes I agree until I see it"). The pilot plans its approach with the same limit, so it starts braking earlier.
- **Arrival:** within 2 m **and** at most 0.5 m/s. Afterwards, and after `stop()`, the thrusters brake toward zero velocity. There is no snap and no position hold. A normal approach passes the click below 0.5 m/s at most and rests just past it, without coming back; a genuine miss still corrects normally.
- **Static contact (Task #44):** after the one `move_and_slide()`, a slide collision with a `StaticBody3D` (not an `AnimatableBody3D`) qualifies when this tick's attempted planar velocity points into the collision normal (XZ). The first one ends the active order (not through `stop()`; pending commands stay), zeroes velocity and turn rate and logs one line. It also applies while coasting or braking without an order. Other body types keep Godot's default sliding.
- **Bad profile:** a missing profile, a missing rudder curve, a missing directional thrust curve, any numeric value ≤ 0 or a negative `reaction_delay` or `no_go_tolerance` logs an error and the ship does not move.

**How it navigates** (`Movement/Navigation/`, Tasks #46, #48 and #50; details in `ARCHITECTURE.md`):
- **No-go zone (Task #48, owner Option A):** the asteroid's baked exclusion is now 19.75 m from its centre (agent radius 13.75 m, +3 m), and ordinary transit keeps the ship's **centre** outside it: routes go around it, a click inside it is resolved to a point outside it (Task #54, below), and a predicted maneuver, typically a fast redirect, whose centre would go more than `no_go_tolerance` (0.5 m) beyond its edge is rejected; if nothing else passes, the order is declined and the ship brakes. The hull may swing into the zone while turning. It is not a wall: from rest the pilot can cut the zone's polygon corners by up to about 0.5 m.
- **One plan per order:** when a move activates (after the reaction delay), one planning job runs. It finishes in the same physics tick for an ordinary order; otherwise the ship brakes until it finishes (a few ticks). Nothing is re-planned while flying; a new order or a stop cancels an unfinished job.
- **Route:** Godot's navigation path from the ship to the click on the baked mesh (13.75 m clearance around the asteroid since Task #48).
- **Safety check:** wherever the ship's hull could touch something along the route (and while the ship is still settling onto it), the plan predicts the flight tick by tick with the real pilot and the real collision box, rotation included. A touching ship may slide away but not swing a corner in.
- **Choices:** the straight order, the route, or a departure that keeps the hull's heading (no turning) and brakes (when already moving), backs off, slides sideways or moves ahead for 0.5–4 s (profile `departure_step_time` / `departure_max_time`) before following the route. The first safe one flies; otherwise the order is declined (the ship brakes and the Output says why).
- **Redirecting a moving ship (Task #50):** the safety check now predicts the ship's whole way back to its route instead of stopping once the route ahead looks clear, and a plan made while the ship kept braking is handed over only on the exact frame its prediction started from (the ship is held braking for those few extra ticks; if its state differs, planning starts again). A straight order uses the same course in the prediction as in flight. A fast redirect near the asteroid that cannot be proven safe is **declined** (the ship brakes, F3 and the Output give the reason) instead of being approved and hitting the rock.
- **Following:** the pilot aims 10 m ahead along the route, slows before each bend so it can hold the curve, and flies the final approach as an ordinary arrival, keeping the heading it has then (no final turn).
- **Destination resolution (Task #54):** a click inside a no-go zone (on or near an asteroid), or one the ship's path cannot reach, is replaced by the nearest suitable point a bounded local search finds; a valid click is untouched and costs nothing extra. Candidates, nearest to the click first: the nearest navigable point, rings of 8 points around the click whose radius doubles from 11.2 m (generated only when nearer candidates have failed, up to the navigation area's bounds), and the end of any failed path. A candidate is moved about 2.08 m in from the zone edge (where the frigate can come to rest after arrival), must keep that whole resting circle outside the zone and clear of physics at any heading, and must be reachable by path. The ship then routes and flies there as to any click. If nothing passes, the order is declined and the ship brakes: "destination search exhausted" (16 path tests used) or "no destination found in bounded search" (the area's bounds reached); a failed maneuver search says "maneuver planning failed" and never tries another point. F3 shows the active (resolved) destination with the requested click beside it, and the order line says how far away it resolved; the Output lists every tested candidate with its reason and the query counts. The magenta marker still marks the click.
- **Clicks beyond the navigation mesh's outer edge (13.75 m inside the 800 × 800 m floor) but outside the region's bounds fly straight, as before, and stop on contact if that line crosses the asteroid.
- **Planning time:** all ships share 1.5 ms per physics tick, preparation included (work runs in small items and stops before one would overrun); one order may predict at most 120 s of flight over all its candidates (`Movement/Navigation/navigation_budget.tres`), counted in predicted time so the same click from the same state always gives the same result.
- **F3 order status:** the readout's `order:` line shows the latest command's outcome: planning, accepted (direct / route / departure), declined with its reason, arrived, stopped or static-contact stop. It stays after the order ends. Timings are in seconds, so behaviour does not depend on the physics tick rate.

## Accepted decisions

- 1 world unit = 1 metre; authoritative gameplay movement uses X/Z.
- Movement is the only top-level gameplay module and may use Godot APIs only. It owns open-space position, world planar velocity and yaw; pilot guidance and propulsion are internal. Task #46 returned path queries and route corners to it, and Task #54 destination resolution, all inside its navigation.
- **Movement v3 rewrite (Task #27, approved ticket and plan revision 1):**
  - **No adaptations:** a rewrite, not an adaptation of the old locomotion.
  - **No mass-derived extras:** no mass-derived throttle ramp and no mass-derived braking optimism.
  - **Main engine:** pushes along the bow only and never brakes.
  - **Thrusters:** one vector budget `thruster_strength / mass` shared by braking and crabbing. Replaced by the owner-approved speed-dependent directional-thrust task: sideways thrust (full budget × curve) and bow-forward thrust (`forward_directional_acceleration` × curve, below the main engine) fade with actual speed through `directional_thrust_curve`; sternward keeps the full budget; braking is served before sideways correction; the approach plans with the braking actually available against the travel direction.
  - **Forward:** forward thrust stacks with the engine (Option A).
  - **Braking:** only the thrusters stop the ship in this slice.
  - **After arrival or `stop()`:** no position hold.
  - **Invalid profiles:** fail closed.
- **Powered turning (Task #28, approved ticket and plan revision 2):**
  - **Heading target:** the intended travel of the order (owner D1), held as one **course heading per order** set by `move_to()` (owner choice in PR #32, replacing the per-tick desired-velocity target that caused the late arrival spin). The A1 trial (no turning while braking against motion) was not implemented.
  - **Sharing:** turning and planar thrust share the directional-thruster capacity proportionally; no independent turning, no translation-priority leftovers, no budget coefficient (owner D4).
  - **Shared-capacity planning (owner-approved trial, PR #32):** the approach speed plans braking at `a_t / (1 + f_r)` and the yaw counter-turn plans at `α_max / (1 + f_t_prev)`. One extra float per ship (last tick's planar fraction); no phase, threshold or smoothing.
  - **No unpowered spin:** "Rotation should not persist without thrust." The turn rate drops to 0 in one tick when powered turning ends (heading continuous); no angular drag.
  - **Authority:** from actual speed through the rudder curve; zero at rest. One speed-linked turn-rate limit (owner-approved trial, PR #32); no phases, angle classes or reversal threshold. No final alignment.
- **Banking:** deferred; the old bank files were deleted with owner approval (Task #27). Visual tilt must never affect the authoritative root or collision.
- **Reaction delay (Task #30, approved ticket as corrected by the tech lead's audit, plan revision 1):**
  - **Rule:** every command, move and stop, waits its own `reaction_delay` from receipt; the old order stands until then; a later command never cancels an earlier pending one; due commands apply in receipt order.
  - **Stop:** delayed like moves, no emergency bypass (owner: "Yes").
  - **Activation:** a move takes its course from the ship's position at activation; the pilot and propulsion are unchanged.
  - **Default:** 0.3 s (owner's veteran-crew example) is a provisional trial; 3.0 s (green crew) is only the decisive-check override.
