**How it moves** (`Movement/ship_movement.gd`, `ShipMovement`):
- **Reaction delay:** `move_to()` and `stop()` only receive a command into one first-in-first-out queue, due `reaction_delay` (0.3 s) later on Movement's physics clock. At the start of each physics tick every due command activates in receipt order: a move sets the destination and the course from where the ship is then; a stop ends the order (ordinary braking). Nothing is cancelled or skipped, and stop has no bypass. Delay 0 acts on the next tick.
- **One velocity:** one world planar velocity and one heading.
- **Frigate values (owner, commit 97ecab7):** mass 12, main acceleration 0.5 m/s², thrusters 40 (40 / 12 = 3.33 m/s²); the turning, directional and bow-alignment curves below; the rest are script defaults (15 m/s, 0.25 s response, arrival 2 m / 0.5 m/s, retro on, 0.45, 0.3 s delay). The numbers below use them.
- **Pilot:** each physics tick it wants to move toward the destination at `sqrt(2 · a_plan · distance) − a_plan · 0.25` m/s, between 0 and 15 (Task #41). Subtracting `a_plan × pilot_response_time` removes the 0.25 s lag of its own response, so it brakes on the ideal stopping curve. Here `a_plan` = the thrusters' reach against the actual travel direction at the actual speed `/ (1 + turning demand fraction)` (bow-first travel: 3.33, so straight braking is unchanged; sideways drift and stern-first travel: the faded sideways limit, 3.33 × the curve), e.g. bow-first: 3.33 m/s² with no turn, 1.67 m/s² while turning takes its full share. Without retro thrusters it is further limited to `1.5 / s`, where `s` is the actual travel direction's share along the bow (when positive): 1.5 m/s² moving bow-first, 3.33 sideways or stern-first. It requests the acceleration that closes the gap to the actual velocity in 0.25 s. **Momentum-preserving course changes:** while moving bow-first, when asking for the new direction at once would brake only because the direction differs (and arrival or a bend does not ask to slow), it instead asks for the travel direction turned toward the new one by the angle the engine and thrusters can turn it in 0.25 s, at the current speed, so the velocity sweeps round; it still slows to the speed at which the turn leaves room to stop at the destination. From rest, stern-first, on the final approach and before route bends it asks for the new direction at once, as before. **Reversal braking:** an order at least 150° (`near_opposite_angle`) from the **actual travel direction** when it activates (bow ignored; the ship faster than 0.5 m/s, the click more than 2 m away) first brakes to 0.5 m/s with the thrusters, holding its heading (no commanded turn), then flies on as an order from rest. Decided once at activation; a new order or stop replaces it.
- **Main engine:** supplies the forward part along the bow, up to 0.5 m/s². It never fires while the ship has any stern-first motion, so it never brakes.
- **Thrusters:** supply the rest from one 40 / 12 = 3.33 m/s² rating, shared with turning (below). **Speed-dependent directional thrust:** sideways thrust is limited to 3.33 × the frigate's `directional_thrust_curve` at the **actual** speed: 100% / 75% / 50% / 25% at 0 / 5 / 10 / 15 m/s (3.33 / 2.50 / 1.67 / 0.83 m/s², linear between, owner-approved provisional). Bow-forward thrust that pushes the ship forward (forward assistance) has its own, smaller limit: `forward_directional_acceleration` 0.25 m/s² × the same curve (0.25 / 0.1875 / 0.125 / 0.0625 m/s²), so the 0.5 m/s² main engine is the primary forward propulsion; bow-forward thrust that brakes stern-first motion keeps 3.33 × the curve. Sternward thrust keeps the full 3.33 m/s² (then the retro cap). Within the planar share, per hull axis, braking against the velocity comes first, sideways maneuvering second and forward assistance last; nothing is scaled up. Momentum is never rotated: at cruise the hull can turn but the ship drifts on.
- **Forward together:** engine and forward thrusters add up to 0.75 m/s² at rest (owner-approved Option A, engine first), falling with the curve to 0.5 + 0.0625 = 0.5625 m/s² at 15 m/s; from rest to 14.9 m/s takes about 23 s and 179 m.
- **Integration:** only applied acceleration is integrated, and the speed is hard-capped at 15 m/s.
- **Turning:** the pilot steers the bow toward the order's **course heading**. For a direct order it starts toward the click when the move activates, then follows the useful direction of travel (the travel direction turned toward the destination by what the ship can turn in 0.25 s; the destination's bearing itself at rest and low speed) until arrival braking begins, and holds from then on, so the hull never pursues an outdated heading and an overshoot never turns it round (no late arrival spin). A new order replaces it; routes set their own course (aim, then frozen final approach). **Bow alignment:** while the ship moves bow-first, the pilot steers toward that course limited to the frigate's `bow_alignment_curve` either side of the actual travel direction: unrestricted at rest, 90° at 5 m/s, 45° at 10 m/s, 30° at 15 m/s (linear between, owner-approved provisional), recalculated every tick, so at speed the hull leads a turn by a bounded angle and the trajectory bends through the engine and thrusters; the hull and velocity are never snapped. Stern-first motion and a standstill are not limited. **Speed-dependent turning:** available turning acceleration and the turn-rate limit are two curves of **actual** speed: 0.5°/s² and 1.8°/s at rest, a peak of 10°/s² and 35°/s at 5 m/s, then 7°/s² and 30°/s at 10 m/s and 3.5°/s² and 20°/s at 15 m/s (linear between, owner-approved provisional): weak in place, tight at low speed, broad and slow at cruise. It winds up and counter-turns with bounded angular acceleration (braking-distance rule), planning the counter-turn on the share it gets beside last tick's thruster demand, `authority / (1 + last planar fraction)`, and, while the ship slows, on the lowest authority and rate limit it will still have before the turn is stopped. Yaw never rotates the velocity. Without an order the turn rate is 0, so rotation never persists without powered turning. At rest a ship with an order turns slowly in place (0.5°/s², up to 1.8°/s).
- **Shared capacity:** thruster and turning demand are fractions of their ratings; when they sum above 1, both are scaled proportionally. At full demand on both, crabbing/braking gets 1.67 m/s² and turning half its authority. The main engine is outside this.
- **Retro thrusters** (`retro_thrusters`, default true): without them, the thrusters' acceleration toward the stern is capped at `0.45 × 3.33 = 1.5 m/s²` after sharing; only the excess sternward part is removed (lateral and bow-forward thrust are unchanged, nothing is scaled up). This weakens braking from bow-first travel **and** stern-first acceleration (owner-provisional: "Yes I agree until I see it"). The pilot plans its approach with the same limit, so it starts braking earlier.
- **Arrival:** within 2 m **and** at most 0.5 m/s. Afterwards, and after `stop()`, the thrusters brake toward zero velocity. There is no snap and no position hold. A normal approach passes the click below 0.5 m/s at most and rests just past it, without coming back; a genuine miss still corrects normally.
- **Static contact (Task #44):** after the one `move_and_slide()`, a slide collision with a `StaticBody3D` (not an `AnimatableBody3D`) qualifies when this tick's attempted planar velocity points into the collision normal (XZ). The first one ends the active order (not through `stop()`; pending commands stay), zeroes velocity and turn rate and logs one line. It also applies while coasting or braking without an order. Other body types keep Godot's default sliding.
- **Bad profile:** a missing profile, a missing turning-acceleration or turn-rate curve, a missing directional thrust curve, any numeric value ≤ 0 or a negative `reaction_delay` or `no_go_tolerance` logs an error and the ship does not move.
